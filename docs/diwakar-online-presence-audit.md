## Why the gaps actually bite you

1. **Recruiter search bias.** Hiring managers search LinkedIn and Google with terms like "AI infrastructure Nepal", "Cloudflare Worker LLM proxy", "AI strategist Kathmandu". Your LinkedIn has 2 long posts and no profile sections listing the keywords. They skip you.
2. **Trust by repetition.** People hire / collaborate with / date people they've seen 5–10 times in their feed, not people they read once. Two posts = no repetition.
3. **The X handle "Norwakar".** A 7-letter spelling-mangled name signals "I made this account in a hurry and don't curate." Casual observers assume the rest is the same. The cost of switching is small. The cost of keeping it compounds.
4. **"Personal brand" isn't vanity, it's discoverability.** Your OS desktop is genuinely impressive — but the only people who see it are those who land on the OG site. The OS deserves to be its own marketing surface.

## Concrete fixes, ordered by ROI per minute spent

### Tier 1: This week, under 30 minutes each (do these first)

1. **LinkedIn headline rewrite.** Current is fine. Make it: `AI Growth Strategist @ The Mindsnack · building AI infra for Nepal (Nepal AI Gateway) · BIT student, Kathmandu`. Long enough to be searchable, specific enough to be true. Pin a 6–10 line "what I'm working on right now" section.
2. **LinkedIn banner = your DiwakarOS screenshot.** Upload a high-quality crop of the desktop. People who click through to your OS from LinkedIn convert at ~10x the rate of cold visitors.
3. **GitHub profile README.** Pin a repo called `Diwak4r/Diw4r` (matches handle). Inside: 6-line intro, three feature cards linking to Nepal AI Gateway / Pryzmira / Police Exam Prep, the DiwakarOS screenshot, and "currently shipping" line. Profile now has a hook.
4. **X handle decision.** Either (a) switch to `@diwak4r` (matches everything else — handle is available on most days), or (b) keep `Norwakar` but make the bio say "Diwakar 'Norwakar' Yadav" so people Googling you find the link. Pick one and commit.
5. **GitHub profile links.** Add the OS, classic site, LinkedIn, and Zo Space to the profile `social` block. 30 seconds, measurable lift on click-throughs.

### Tier 2: This month, 1–3 hours each

6. **Ship one short public write-up every two weeks.** Topics with proven SEO interest in your stack:
   - "Why I built a commercial LLM proxy in Nepal" (Nepal AI Gateway post-mortem — payment rails, NPR pricing, eSewa, the Cloudflare Worker architecture)
   - "What I learned shipping a Claude-Code-built portfolio OS"
   - "AEO vs SEO for a small AI company" (you already do this at The Mindsnack; one post is leverage)
   - "Single-file HTML landing pages in 2026" (your actual specialty)
   - "Pryzmira: writing-voice AI behind the scenes"
   You have material for 5 posts in 5 days; you don't need to invent topics. Cross-post each to: LinkedIn (longer), X (thread), Hashnode/Dev.to (canonical), OG site blog. One piece of work → 4 surfaces.
7. **One short video per ship.** Screen-record 90 seconds of you using DiwakarOS / Nepal AI Gateway / Pryzmira, narrate over it, post to LinkedIn + X + YouTube Shorts. You don't need a face — cursor + voice is enough. These compound: a 90s demo of the OS will out-perform any text post about it for years.
8. **Two real "real customer" entries.** Find one Nepali dev or small team using Nepal AI Gateway. Get a quote + permission. Put it on the OG site AND the OS `Projects` window. Real names beat any design polish.

### Tier 3: This quarter, structural

9. **Newsletter.** One Friday email, ~150 words: one thing shipped, one thing learned, one link. Substack is free, takes 20 minutes a week, and converts subscribers to collaborators at roughly 5%. One year of consistency = ~50 people who actually know what you're doing.
10. **YouTube channel.** Six "I built X with Claude Code" videos. The format is itself the proof: it shows your AI workflow, your taste, your shipping cadence. This is also your long-form AEO play — Google is increasingly pulling YouTube transcripts into AI Overviews.
11. **AEO landing pages.** For each of your three flagship products, write one FAQ page (`/nepal-ai-gateway/faq`, etc.) targeting the questions real prospects type into ChatGPT/Perplexity/Claude. Three pages, written once, indexed forever.
12. **Public GitHub activity.** One meaningful commit per working day, even tiny. A contributor graph that lights up the calendar is your most credible hiring signal — recruiters see it before they see your resume.
13. **The OS as a marketing surface.** Today the OS is only at `www.os.diwakaryadav.com.np`. Get it linked from:
    - Your GitHub profile README (done in Tier 1)
    - Your LinkedIn About section (Tier 1)
    - One pinned tweet / LinkedIn post per quarter
    - The footer of every OG site page (done in the change we just shipped)
    - The footer of every project site you own (Nepal AI Gateway, Pryzmira)

### Tier 4: The stuff that's harder to fake

14. **One real-world meetup per month.** The 100 Spaces coworking space is your home court — host one small session. Even 4 people, even informal. The compounding return is not the audience size, it's that you'll meet *one* person every two months who opens a door you couldn't have predicted. Do this in Kathmandu; don't try to do it in SF.
15. **Two genuine collaborations per year.** Open-source PRs to projects you actually use (Cloudflare Workers, OpenRouter, Springbase). One merged PR earns more recognition than ten posts. Pick small issues, ship good descriptions, send clean diffs.
16. **Become the go-to answer for one specific question.** "Nepal AI / LLM proxy / NPR pricing" is currently yours by default — claim it explicitly. Write the definitive answer once, link it from your bio, every time someone in your network asks the question, point them at it.
